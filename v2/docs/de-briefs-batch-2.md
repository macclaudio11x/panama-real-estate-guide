## The finding that applies to all three pages

**Not one German-language page across roughly 35 examined cites a single Panamanian primary source.** No Ley numbers, no Decretos, no DGI, ATTT or Registro Público links. The two most authoritative German-language sources in the whole market are German government pages (`auswaertiges-amt.de`, `panama.diplo.de`) and neither cites a law either.

A German page that cites instrument numbers would be **the only cited page in the market.** That is the differentiator on all three, and it is the thing this site already does in English.

## Page 2 — `/de/kaufen/beste-stadtteile-panama-stadt`

**There is no German-language page that compares Panama City neighbourhoods at zone level.** The only two structured as a zone-by-zone comparison are machine translations of English source material. Our 8-zone comparison would be the first native German one.

### Corrections available
1. **The top-ranking German page recommends two zones the German government flags.** `arquidiamantina.org` lists Calidonia and "Tocumen Airport Area" among its 8 best areas to live. Both appear on the Auswärtiges Amt's high-robbery-risk list — *"San Miguelito, Tocumen, Juan Díaz, 24 de Diciembre, El Chorrillo, Santa Ana, Curundu, Calidonia und Pacora"* — stamped **17.08.2026**, i.e. current. No German page cites the AA list at all, despite it being the one authoritative German-language safety source.
2. **`panama-individual.com` lists "Santiago" as a Panama City neighbourhood to avoid.** Santiago is a city in Veraguas, roughly 250 km away.
3. **`50plusabroad.ch` puts Boquete at 2,500 m.** The town is around 1,200 m; Volcán Barú is 3,475 m. Consistent with what we published on `/areas/volcan`.
4. **`wohnsitzausland` prices San Francisco and Bella Vista "unter $600"** while pricing cheaper, adjacent El Cangrejo at $700–1,200. Internally inconsistent.

### ⚠️ A structural question for our ENGLISH page too
Verified against the official Junta Comunal (`jcbellavista.gob.pa/barrios`): the **corregimiento of Bella Vista contains** El Cangrejo, Marbella, Bella Vista Viejo, Obarrio, Campo Alegre, La Cresta and El Carmen. Every German page treats Bella Vista, Marbella, Obarrio and El Cangrejo as peers at the same level. **They are not.**

And **Avenida Balboa is not a barrio at all** — it is an avenue/corridor spanning Punta Paitilla, Marbella and Bella Vista. Our English page lists it as one of its eight "zones", and we run a separate `/buying/avenida-balboa-panama-real-estate` article. Worth deciding how both pages frame this rather than inheriting the market's error.

### Vocabulary
**Stadtteil** and **Viertel** are the search terms; **Gegend** in body copy; **Wohngegend**, **Vorort**, **bewachte Wohnanlage** (gated community), **Wohnturm**. Safety: **Sicherheitslage** carries the analysis, plus **meiden** — *"Viertel, die man meiden sollte"* is the exact query shape. *corregimiento* and *barrio* are left in Spanish everywhere and nobody explains the difference.

### Unresolved before writing
**Whether Germans get 90 or 180 days visa-free.** Auswärtiges Amt says 90, stamped today; `50plusabroad` says 180. The 180-day allowance is documented for US/Canadian nationals. **Check Migración before publishing either number.**

## Page 3 — `/de/leben/fuehrerschein-in-panama`

**No German residency consultancy or law firm has a real driving-licence page.** `fischer-cie.de/auswandern-panama/` ranks for the query and does not mention licences at all. The field is one embassy page, three blogs and a dead domain.

### The market's core conceptual error
**"Umschreibung" implies a treaty-based exchange, and DACH has no agreement with Panama.** Spain does — Sertracen runs a dedicated *Canje de licencia de conducir Española* page. German, Austrian and Swiss holders run the generic *primera vez, extranjero con licencia extranjera* trámite. No German page says this, and several imply a homologation treaty exists.

### The genuine scoop, if it holds
Panama's official requirements list **three** certification routes: (A) embassy/consulate in Panama, (B) **apostille of a certification issued by the home licensing authority**, (C) consular endorsement. Panama and all of DACH are Hague members. **Every German page teaches only (A)** — the €96 appointment in Panama City.

⚠️ **Unverified and worth real money to readers if true:** whether a German *Fahrerlaubnisbehörde*, Austrian *BH* or Swiss *Strassenverkehrsamt* will issue that certification in apostillable form. Confirm before publishing.

### The DACH facts nobody has written
- **There is no Swiss embassy in Panama.** The consulate in Panama City has no notarial powers; certification runs through the Swiss embassy in **San José, Costa Rica**.
- **There is no Austrian embassy either.** Honorary General Consulate in Panama City, accredited embassy in **Bogotá** since March 2016; BMEIA notes Panamanian documents need an Apostille. **Nobody has written what an Austrian actually does.**

### Other corrections
- **"90 days" is presented everywhere as the validity of the German licence. It is not** — it attaches to tourist status, and the same provision separately bars tourists from obtaining a Panamanian licence or an extension. So a tourist past 90 days cannot legally drive *and* cannot fix it by applying. Missing everywhere.
- **The "no test" claim needs a category caveat.** True for car categories. Sertracen requires theory and practical exams plus a drug test and health certificate for D, E2, E3, F and G — so anyone converting a German C/CE truck entitlement is misled by every German page.
- **`fincabayano` expands SERTRACEN as "Servicio de Tránsito y Transporte Terrestre."** Sertracen is a private concessionaire, not a state service. Looks fabricated.
- **ATTT vs Sertracen:** the official trámite names ATTT as issuer with Sertracen running the counters. Word this as a precision point, not a debunk — **the German embassy itself calls Sertracen "die zuständige panamaische Stelle."**
- Embassy fee spread: **€96** (embassy, authoritative) vs ~$120 vs ~$100 vs USD 56.
- **The residency cliff is probably overstated.** Every German page says the foreign licence dies the instant residency is granted; Sertracen's own canje page assumes you present a *valid* foreign licence while already holding a carné. Whether a grace window exists is unresolved. Be the page that says so.

⚠️ Article numbering (DE 640/2006 arts. 110/111) is secondhand via vlex and panamadigital because `transito.gob.pa` fails TLS. Confirm before citing.

### Vocabulary
**umschreiben / Umschreibung** holds the volume. **Überbeglaubigung** is the embassy's own word and the exact term a reader searches next. **Führerschein** not *Fahrerlaubnis*. **Scheckkartenformat** is load-bearing — the embassy's distinction is card-format yes, old paper no. Carry **Führerausweis / Fahrausweis** once for Swiss readers.

## Page 4 — `/de/kaufen/immobilienmarkt-panama`

Our most commercially valuable German page, and the one with the biggest error to correct.

### 🚩 The correction: who pays the 2% transfer tax

`fischer-cie.de` — a German-language page authored by a **licensed Panamanian attorney** (Karen Dorcy, #10179), ranking top-3 — puts the **2% inside the buyer's cost stack**, computes it as $6,000 on a $300,000 purchase, and rolls it into a $308,900 buyer total. `vidalibreplan` folds "2 Prozent Grunderwerbsteuer (ITBI)" into a buyer's 4–7% closing range.

Both are wrong, and doubly so. Our own sourced English position: **the transfer tax is 2% of land or cadastral value, and the seller files it, not the buyer** (DGI Form 106). So the German market's leading legal page has the wrong party *and* the wrong base — cadastral value on an older property frequently sits well below the purchase price.

`immogration` and `frapan` both get it right (buyer 0%, seller pays), so the German market contradicts itself on the single largest line item a buyer budgets.

### The most consequential unflagged error
**Every German page presents the $120,000 property-tax exemption as automatic.** It is *Patrimonio Familiar Tributario*: primary residence only, and it must be applied for. A buyer of a second home or investment unit starts paying at **$30,001** — a 4× difference in where tax begins.

Compounding it: the circulating brackets ("0.5% on $120,001–250,000, 0.7% above") are a **hybrid of the two real Ley 66 de 2017 ladders and match neither**, and the **0.8% and 1.0% bands that most foreign investment buyers land in are silently dropped**. A $600,000 non-primary-residence condo sits in the 0.8–1.0% band and no German page says so.

### Other correction targets
- **Capital gains: three numbers, no mechanism.** "3%", "10%", and silence. Only `immogration` frames it correctly as a 3% presumptive advance with a 10%-on-actual-gain election.
- **Escrow: flat contradiction.** `fischer-cie` prices it at 0.25–1% as normal; `panamajetzt` says *"Notarkonten sind in Panama nicht üblich."* Nobody names a bank, agent or legal basis.
- **Yields spread 0% to 9.3%; one page shows its work.** `frapan`'s undated, uncited "~7% gross" and "$2,500/m²" appear to be the origin of figures repeated across the niche. `immogration`'s own worked cases land at **4.57% net** on a long-term Panama City rental. The "6.2%–9.3%" figure has the false precision of a fabricated range.
- **Mortgage financing for foreigners: zero data anywhere.** No rate, no LTV, no term, no bank named. Complete vacuum on a high-intent question.
- **`wohin-auswandern.de` (2017, still ranking on the head term)** carries ITBMS at 5% (it is 7%), conflates the transfer tax with the annual tax, and presents the legacy pre-Ley 66 20-year construction exemption as current.
- **Casco Antiguo incentives are absent from German entirely**, and the Ley 519 de 2026 reset is nowhere — see `de-brief` material and the Casco research.
- **Ley 41 art. 59** never appears: nobody addresses the belief that residency status brings property-tax relief.

### Two things NOT to do
1. **Do not lead on "property doesn't buy you residency."** `fassnachtpanama.substack.com` already owns that argument in German, dated 04.06.2026, with a CFA byline.
2. **Do not compete on €/m² bands** — they are all asking-price fiction. Fassnacht's **$1,987/m²** on a real closed transaction ($377,000, ~190 m², San Francisco, HOA $320/month) is the only honest per-m² number in the German market, and beating it needs closed-transaction data we do not have.

### Vocabulary
**Kaufnebenkosten** is the money keyword. **Grunderwerbsteuer** is what searchers type for the 2% — but it is a German tax concept mapped onto a Panamanian *seller's* tax, so using it uncritically imports the error. Prefer **Übertragungssteuer** and explain the mismatch. **Nettorendite vs Bruttorendite** matters: German buyers arrive expecting net and every page except one hands them gross. *Derecho posesorio* is left in Spanish with no German term in circulation — *Besitzrecht* does not appear anywhere, which is convenient, because our glossary forbids it.

## The Austria / Switzerland opening, across all three

Germany-default everywhere; Switzerland gets accidental coverage; **Austria is essentially unserved on all three topics.**

`wohnsitzausland.com` is the only page in the market with genuine per-country exit-tax treatment — German *Wegzugsbesteuerung* (§ 6 AStG) and *erweiterte beschränkte Steuerpflicht* (§ 2 AStG), Austrian exit taxation at 27.5%, Swiss capital-gains exemption. But it is a tax page with no property content, while the property pages have no tax content. **Nobody connects the halves.**

Specifically absent everywhere:
- **Switzerland taxes worldwide *wealth*, so a Panamanian property enters the cantonal *Vermögenssteuer* base.** No German-language Panama page mentions this recurring consequence.
- Austrian **Immobilienertragsteuer** interaction, and the Austria–Panama treaty position.
- Whether DE, AT or CH has a double-taxation treaty covering immovable property, and what happens to Panamanian rental income under **Progressionsvorbehalt**. First question a tax-aware DACH buyer asks; the answer is nowhere in German.
- **CHF appears nowhere.** Nobody addresses USD exposure for a CHF- or EUR-earning buyer — which is the same gap our `retire-in-panama` page already fills for pensions.
